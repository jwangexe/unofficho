In an octahedral complex, six ligands sit along the $\pm x$, $\pm y$ and $\pm z$ axes. The five d orbitals no longer have equal energy. The ligands' electron pairs repel electrons in some orbitals more than in others.

## Two sets of orbitals

- **$e_g$** ($d_{z^2}$, $d_{x^2-y^2}$) point straight at the ligands. They are pushed up.
- **$t_{2g}$** ($d_{xy}$, $d_{xz}$, $d_{yz}$) point between the ligands. They are pushed down.

The gap is $\Delta_\mathrm{o}$. The splitting keeps the average energy unchanged (the barycentre), so the two sets sit at

$$
E(t_{2g}) = -0.4\,\Delta_\mathrm{o}, \qquad E(e_g) = +0.6\,\Delta_\mathrm{o}
$$

The crystal field stabilisation energy for $x$ electrons in $t_{2g}$ and $y$ in $e_g$ is then

$$
\text{CFSE} = (-0.4x + 0.6y)\,\Delta_\mathrm{o}
$$

plus a pairing energy $P$ for each extra pair compared with the free ion.

## High spin or low spin?

For d¹–d³ and d⁸–d¹⁰ there is only one way to fill the orbitals. For **d⁴–d⁷** the fourth electron has a choice:

- if $\Delta_\mathrm{o} < P$, it goes up into $e_g$, giving **high spin** with the most unpaired electrons
- if $\Delta_\mathrm{o} > P$, it pairs in $t_{2g}$, giving **low spin**

For example, Fe²⁺ (d⁶) gives:

| Complex | Field | Configuration | Unpaired | CFSE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | weak | $t_{2g}^4e_g^2$ | 4 | $-0.4\,\Delta_\mathrm{o}$ |
| [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ | strong | $t_{2g}^6$ | 0 | $-2.4\,\Delta_\mathrm{o} + 2P$ |

The spin-only magnetic moment $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\,\mu_\mathrm{B}$ separates them: 4.90 $\mu_\mathrm{B}$ against zero.

## What makes $\Delta_\mathrm{o}$ large

1. **The ligand.** The spectrochemical series runs from weak to strong field:
   I⁻ < Br⁻ < Cl⁻ < F⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < en < CN⁻ < CO.
   π-donors sit at the weak end. π-acceptors sit at the strong end.
2. **Higher oxidation state of the metal.** M³⁺ splits more than M²⁺.
3. **Going down a group.** 4d and 5d orbitals are larger and overlap better. Their complexes are almost always low spin.

<Callout type="trap">
Count d electrons **after** removing the 4s electrons. Fe²⁺ is [Ar]3d⁶, not [Ar]3d⁴4s². The transition metal loses its 4s electrons first.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
Tetrahedral splitting is smaller, about $\Delta_\mathrm{t} \approx \tfrac{4}{9}\Delta_\mathrm{o}$, and the order is inverted (e below t₂). It is almost never larger than $P$. So tetrahedral first-row complexes are high spin unless a question says otherwise.
</Callout>
