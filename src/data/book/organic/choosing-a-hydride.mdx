Hydride reagents are not interchangeable. Their selectivity follows from two things: how **nucleophilic** the hydride is, and whether the reagent is **Lewis acidic** enough to activate the carbonyl itself.

## The nucleophilic hydrides

<R id="lialh4" /> is reactive because Al–H is weaker and more polarised than B–H, and Li⁺ coordinates the carbonyl oxygen. It reduces nearly every carbonyl: aldehydes, ketones, esters, acids (after deprotonation), amides (to amines) and nitriles (to amines).

<R id="nabh4" /> is milder and works in alcohols or water. It reduces aldehydes and ketones but leaves esters, acids and amides essentially alone:

## Stopping at the aldehyde

<R id="dibal-h">DIBAL-H</R> is a neutral, Lewis-acidic aluminium hydride. At −78 °C it adds once to an ester. The resulting aluminium hemiacetal survives until work-up, which releases the aldehyde:

<Scheme
  from="CCCCC(=O)OC"
  to="CCCCC=O"
  reagent="dibal-h"
  under="−78 °C; then H₂O"
  fromLabel="Methyl pentanoate"
  toLabel="Pentanal"
/>

Use one equivalent and keep the reaction cold. Warmer conditions or excess reagent go on to the alcohol.

## Electrophilic borane

<R id="bh3-thf">Borane</R> works the other way round. It is electron-poor, so it seeks electron-rich carbonyl oxygens. Carboxylic acids react fastest, via an acyloxyborane, and amides are reduced to amines. Esters are slow. This makes BH₃ the reagent for reducing an acid next to an ester, the reverse of what NaBH₄ would do.

| Reagent | Aldehyde / ketone | Ester | Acid | Amide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NaBH₄ | alcohol | slow / none | none | none |
| LiAlH₄ | alcohol | 1° alcohol | 1° alcohol | amine |
| DIBAL-H, −78 °C | alcohol | aldehyde | — | — |
| BH₃·THF | alcohol | slow | 1° alcohol | amine |

<Callout type="trap">
"LiAlH₄ reduces amides" means the C=O becomes CH₂, giving an **amine**, not an alcohol. It is a common slip to draw the amino alcohol or the alcohol instead.
</Callout>

## Tuned reagents

- <R id="nabh3cn" /> is stable at pH 6–7. At that pH it reduces protonated iminium ions much faster than ketones, which is why it is used for reductive amination.
- <R id="l-selectride" /> is very bulky. On substituted cyclohexanones it attacks from the less hindered equatorial direction, giving the **axial** alcohol.

<Callout type="tip">
To choose a reagent in a synthesis question, list every reducible group in the molecule first. Then pick the mildest reagent that reduces the one you want, rather than the strongest one that will.
</Callout>
